The Goal-Question-Metric (GQM) framework is a highly structured approach used in software engineering research to ensure that every single piece of data you collect actually serves a specific, measurable purpose. It prevents the common research trap of asking interesting but ultimately useless questions.

Here is how the hierarchy works:

    Goal (Conceptual level): What specifically are you trying to understand, evaluate, or improve?

    Question (Operational level): What questions do you need to ask to achieve that goal?

    Metric (Quantitative level): What specific, measurable data point will answer that question?

Right now, you have a great list of Questions, and the Metrics are implied by the way you intend to collect the answers (e.g., percentages, 1-5 scales). To use GQM effectively in your paper, you need to work backward slightly and define the formal Goals that justify why these questions exist.

Why GQM is Crucial for Your Paper

When you write the "Methodology" section of your research paper, explicitly mentioning that you used the GQM approach will instantly elevate its academic rigor.

Instead of saying, "I asked them these questions to see what they do," you can say: "The survey was designed using the Goal-Question-Metric (GQM) framework to ensure empirical validity. We established four primary goals regarding developer demographics, tooling, project management, and quality assurance, and derived targeted questions and measurable data points for each."

This proves to your readers (or graders) that every question on your Google Form is carrying its weight and directly contributing to your thesis.
